9,362 homes, and lots that face something

Plenty of the 9,362 homes in Acworth look at water or at open ground, not at another back yard. A fence built to screen a neighbor and a fence built to contain a dog without blocking a view are two completely different briefs, and Acworth produces more of the second than anywhere else we work.

A choice worth making slowly

Acworth was half built by 2000, and the fences that went up with those houses are reaching the age where the original comes down. On a lot with something worth looking at, solid wood privacy is the wrong reflex, and aluminum in an open picket does the containment job without taking the view away. On an interior lot with neighbors on three sides the reverse is true. Same town, opposite answers.

Humidity and what it does to wood

Ground within reach of still water holds moisture longer, and wood in contact with moist ground gives up at ground level first. That's not a reason to avoid wood near the lakes. It's a reason to be deliberate about what happens where the post meets the ground and how often the fence gets sealed, because near water the interval is shorter than the label suggests.
