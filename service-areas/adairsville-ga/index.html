Working in the town we live in

Just under 5,000 people live inside the city limits, spread across about 1,665 homes, and 1,020 of those are lived in by the people who own them. That is a small enough number that word of mouth still decides who gets called. Send the details and a couple of photos and we will tell you what it needs.

Adairsville decks and the 2001 problem

The median Adairsville house was finished in 2001. A deck framed at the same time is now at the age where the connection points loosen well before the decking itself looks bad, so what fails first is a ledger, a stair stringer or a rail post, not the surface you can see. That makes Adairsville a repair-and-rebuild market rather than a demolition one. Read what a new deck build involves, or what deck repair and replacement covers when the frame is still worth keeping.

Fencing on a small-town lot

Adairsville is a small city of roughly 1,665 homes, which means shorter street frontages and more walk gates than drive gates, and the detail on that is on the Adairsville fence page rather than here, with materials covered by the fence company page.

Shade structures where 71.6 percent of homes are owner occupied

Seven homes in ten here are owner occupied. That is the highest ownership share of any town we cover outside Powder Springs, and it matters because nobody puts a cedar post in the ground for the benefit of the next tenant. A pergola or a solid patio cover is a purchase made by somebody planning to still be sitting under it a long way from now, and Adairsville has more of those households per hundred than almost anywhere we work.

Screened porches without a lake to blame

There is no named lake or river inside the Adairsville boundary. The reason to screen a porch here is the ordinary one: summer evenings in north Bartow are the part of the year you actually want the room for, and screen is what makes them usable. See how the three levels of enclosure compare on the screened porch page.

410 feet of rise inside the city limits

Adairsville runs from 671 feet to 1,081 feet. Four hundred and ten feet of change in a town this size means a good share of back yards sit on a grade steep enough that the usable flat area has to be built rather than found. That is what a retaining wall is for, and on a lot like that the wall usually comes before the patio, not after it.

Where the water goes: Gunn Creek and Oothkalooga Creek

Two named streams run through town, Gunn Creek and Oothkalooga Creek. Both of them tell you which way the ground drains, and a patio is a shed surface before it is anything else. Getting the fall right on a patio, walkway or set of steps is what keeps water moving past the house instead of standing against the slab.

Where a fire pit ends up

Siting is a conversation about your lot, prevailing wind and how far the seating sits from the house, and it happens on site. The build itself sits with patios and hardscape, and there are twenty layouts worth arguing about in the fire pit guide.
