Deck Builder in Ball Ground, GA

A small town on high ground where almost everybody owns. Ball Ground is the longest of our four Cherokee County runs, and we have built a pool deck here.

About 62 minutes and 46.1 road miles. Just over half of that is interstate, I-75 down to Knox Bridge Highway and I-575 back up, and it is the longest of the four Cherokee County runs.

Two thousand eight hundred people at the top of Cherokee County

Ball Ground holds 2,856 people in 1,188 homes, 982 of them owner occupied. It is much the smallest of the four Cherokee towns we cover and the farthest of them from the shop, and there is a finished pool deck here already. If you would rather skip the reading, send a few pictures and the dimensions.

2007 housing, and nineteen summers on the surface

Take a Ball Ground house at random and it was probably finished around 2007. A deck framed alongside a house that age has generally not moved, but what you walk on and what you lean on have absorbed nineteen Georgia summers of sun and standing wet, so the useful conversation starts with boards, rails and fasteners rather than with the structure under them. That is repair and replacement of the parts that actually wore out, and washing and sealing what is left is what buys the rest of it more time. Where somebody wants more deck than the builder ever left, a new build is the other road.

1,188 homes, and a fence line the neighbors have half decided

This is a small town and 1,188 homes is the whole of it. At that size a run is usually bounded by what the people either side already stood up, rather than by one pattern repeated down an entire street, so what gets built has to meet two existing lines and still look deliberate. Where the yard ends is worth agreeing on with both of them before anybody digs. Material against material, the fence company page is where that argument lives.

85.2 percent of the occupied homes belong to the people inside them

Of the 1,153 occupied homes, 982 have an owner living inside them. Hardly anywhere else we work comes close. A beam and a set of posts is a twenty year decision, and twenty year decisions get made by the people who expect to be standing under them. That is why a pergola, or a patio cover with a real roof on it, does well in a town shaped like this one.

No lake, no pond, no river inside the town

Nothing named sits inside the Ball Ground line. That leaves a screened porch here to argue for itself, and it manages: screen is what turns a covered slab from something you look at into a room you can still eat in after dark. What screen, a roof above the screen and full glass each cost you in light and air is worked through on that page.

Nothing in Ball Ground sits below 941 feet

The lowest ground in Ball Ground is 941 feet, the highest is 1,261, and it averages 1,103. Three hundred and twenty feet of change on ground that never drops low means the fall runs off shoulders and ridges instead of down into a bottom, which is a friendlier place to build than it sounds. A retaining wall on a shoulder gets a purchase at both ends. A wall that has to stand alone across the middle of a flat yard does not.

No named creek crosses this town at all

Most towns around here have a creek or a branch running through them somewhere, and Ball Ground has neither. With no channel on the map to read off, the fall under a patio, a walkway or a run of steps has to come off what somebody measures standing in the yard, which is where it should have come from anyway.

A fire pit needs a decision before it needs a ring

What a fire pit really wants is a patch of ground somebody has already chosen to sit on, and that choice gets made in person, standing there, usually in the evening. Everything else follows it. The construction is hardscape. Shapes get compared in the guide to fire pits.

Ball Ground is an hour out. Do you actually come this far?

Yes, for work that fills a day rather than an hour of one. We have built a pool deck in this town, so the drive has been made and it will be made again for the right job.

Is a deck around a pool built differently from any other deck?

The pool sets the shape and it sets where people walk, which is the reverse of a back yard deck where the door decides both. The surface also stays wet far more of the time. Both of those change the layout well before anybody picks a board.

The house dates to around 2007. Is the deck due?

Probably not the frame. On housing that age what gives out is what you walk on and what you lean on. Pictures of the boards, the stair and the rail usually settle it without anybody driving up.
