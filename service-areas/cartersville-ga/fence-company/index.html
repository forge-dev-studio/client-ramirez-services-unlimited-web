Cartersville splits cleanly in two: old platting near the center, modern subdivision grid on the outside, and the fence job is different in each.

10,161 homes and two kinds of lot

Cartersville built its 10,161 homes in two goes. The older part of the city was platted long before anybody was drawing cul-de-sacs, so the lots there are irregular, the boundaries don't always meet at right angles, and a fence has to be laid out, not simply measured. The newer subdivisions are the opposite: predictable rectangles, standard side yards, and a run length you can estimate over the phone.

Fences either side of thirty, and a genuine material choice

Cartersville houses fall either side of 1990, which puts a lot of original fences at the point where they're neither fine nor finished. That's the moment people actually compare materials, not defaulting, so it's worth the comparison: wood privacy for screening and cost, or vinyl and aluminum where the maintenance is the deciding factor. Each fails differently and neither is the right answer everywhere.

Slope, and why a Cartersville fence steps

This is the steepest place in Bartow County, and a fence crossing falling ground has to either follow the grade or step down it. Following the grade keeps a continuous line and works with wood. Stepping keeps every panel level and leaves triangles at the bottom that either get filled or don't. Which one suits depends on how fast the ground drops, and it's a decision worth making before anything is ordered instead of on the day.

Should a fence follow the slope or step down it?

Following the slope suits wood and keeps a clean top line. Stepping suits panels and preformed sections and leaves gaps at the bottom that need filling. How steep the ground is decides it.
