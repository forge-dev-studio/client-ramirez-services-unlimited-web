4,050 homes on lots nobody drew from a template

Cedartown was laid out long before anybody was planning subdivisions, and its 4,050 homes, the second fewest of the sixteen towns we cover, sit on lots that are not rectangles. Boundaries meet at angles nobody planned, and back lines follow something that used to be there. A fence here is set out on the ground with string and a tape rather than read off a plan, and that is time before it is materials.

The chain link that is already there

1974 sits in the middle of the Cedartown houses, tied with Rome for the oldest of any town we work in, so most fence work here involves something that is already up. Usually that is chain link, often original to the house, and often in better shape at the posts than the fabric would suggest. Before agreeing to a full replacement it is worth checking whether repair gets you where you want to be, because on a fence this old the terminals are frequently the only part that was ever done properly.

Angles cost more than length

On irregular platting the corner count is the number that matters. A straight two hundred foot run is a straightforward day. The same two hundred feet with seven changes of direction is a different job entirely, because every one of those is a braced post that has to resist pull from two directions at once. It is the reason a Cedartown estimate and a Woodstock estimate for the same footage rarely match.

The old chain link has rusted but the posts seem fine. Worth saving?

Often yes. If the terminal and line posts are solid at ground level, new fabric on the existing frame is a fraction of the work of starting again.
