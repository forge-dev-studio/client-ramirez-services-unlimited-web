10,227 people in the oldest housing on the list

4,050 homes and 1,615 of them owner occupied. Cedartown has the lowest ownership share of any hub at 43.3 percent and, tied with Rome, the oldest housing anywhere in the footprint. That combination produces a very particular kind of work.

A 1974 median, and boards that have earned their retirement

Half the houses here were finished before 1974. Any deck that came with the house has been replaced, patched or abandoned by now, and the ones still standing are usually held up by framing that was never designed for the way it has been loaded since. The starting question is whether there is anything worth keeping, which is what deck repair and replacement is for, and the railing and stair is nearly always the part that fails a safety look first.

4,050 homes on older lots

Cedartown is a mid-sized incorporated place with 4,050 housing units, mostly older platting with irregular lot shapes rather than modern subdivision grid, and what that does to a fence line is the subject of the Cedartown fence page, with materials on the fence company page.

43.3 percent owner occupied, the lowest of the sixteen

Fewer than half of occupied Cedartown homes are owner occupied. That makes it the thinnest pergola and patio cover market on this list and the page is not going to dress that up. Where the work does happen it is in the settled streets, and it tends to be a cover over an existing slab rather than a new structure on new ground.

Vance Lake and Springdale Lake

Two named lakes sit inside the Cedartown boundary. Both are still water, which is the kind that drives insect pressure hardest, so a screened porch here is doing real work rather than adding a feature. On an older house the porch structure often already exists and only needs screening properly, which is the cheapest of the three routes on that page.

197 feet across the boundary

Cedartown runs 766 to 963 feet, 197 feet of change and the third flattest hub. Flat and old is a patio and deck-replacement profile rather than a wall profile, and this page says that rather than talking up retaining wall work that most Cedartown lots do not need. Where a wall does go in it is usually low and decorative.

Cedar Creek and Lake Creek

Only two named streams cross the boundary, the fewest of any hub alongside Adairsville. On flat ground with few defined channels, water leaves slowly, and a hard surface put down without a deliberate fall will hold it. Getting the slope right is most of the job on a Cedartown patio or walkway.
