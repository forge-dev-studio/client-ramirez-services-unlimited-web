Cherokee sits in a 48 to 62 minute band, from Canton at 35.3 road miles to Ball Ground at 46.1.

22,543 owner-occupied homes, nearly all of them recent

Cherokee brings 37,126 housing units and 22,543 owner-occupied households, second only to Cobb. What makes it unusual is not the size but the uniformity: four measured places, every one of them with a housing median inside a five year window.

2005 to 2009, and why the usual argument does not apply

Woodstock and Canton both sit at 2005, Ball Ground at 2007 and Holly Springs at 2009. There is nothing in this county old enough to be at the end of its service life, so a page telling Cherokee homeowners their decks are failing would be selling something that is not true. What is true is that these are first-deck, first-fence and first-patio yards: building new rather than repairing, and picking a surface with a clean sheet.

Three pages and one named town

Woodstock, Canton and Holly Springs each carry a page. Ball Ground, at 982 owner-occupied homes, is named here instead. It is 46.1 road miles out, the furthest point in the county, and at 85.2 percent owner occupied it is one of the most settled places in the whole file. It is also the only market anywhere in the data with no named stream inside its boundary at all, which is worth stating precisely because it is unusual.

231 feet to 396 feet

Relief across Cherokee runs 231 feet at Woodstock to 396 at Canton, with Ball Ground at 320 and Holly Springs at 293. That is a consistently sloped county with no flat end, and on new subdivisions the cut and fill from construction usually leaves a steep back third. Retaining walls are frequently the first thing a Cherokee County yard needs, before any deck or patio can go in.

The Little River, one reservoir, and two places with nothing named

The Little River sits inside Woodstock and Hickory Log Creek Reservoir inside Canton. Holly Springs and Ball Ground carry no named water at all. That means the screened porch case is genuinely stronger in the two northern markets than in the two southern ones, and this page says so rather than flattening it. Blankets Creek, Noonday Creek and Avery Creek set the drainage lines patios and walkways have to follow.

Four incorporated places, all platted subdivision

Every Cherokee place is incorporated and the layout is modern throughout, which produces the most predictable fence work in the footprint: consistent run lengths, square corners and a standard gate count. Predictable is good. It means the estimate and the finished job match. Each market carries its own fence page: Woodstock, Canton and Holly Springs.

55.6 to 85.2 percent owner occupied

Canton sits at 55.6 percent and Ball Ground at 85.2, with Woodstock and Holly Springs between them. A high ownership county full of new houses with bare yards is the strongest pergola and patio cover profile there is, because there is nothing out there yet and the people deciding are the people staying.

The limits of what is known

Nothing in the Cherokee record predicts fire pit placement, so this page claims none; the build sits with hardscape. No Cherokee County job has been photographed for the site. Work that has been shot is on the gallery, the whole footprint is on the service areas page, and the contact page is where a job starts.

My house is only fifteen years old. Do I need any of this?

Probably not repair work. What most Cherokee County houses need is the thing the builder did not include, which is usually a bigger deck, a patio or a wall to make the back of the lot usable.

Is Ball Ground covered?

Yes, at 46.1 road miles. It has no page because 982 owner-occupied homes is below the threshold this site uses for a page of its own.
