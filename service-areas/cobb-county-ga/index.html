Nine towns, one county

Deck and Fence Builder in Cobb County, GA

Cobb is not one market. It runs from 36.9 percent owner occupied to 82.4 percent, and from 121 feet of grade to 315, inside the same county line.

Cobb County spans a 37 to 71 minute band, from Acworth at 29.7 road miles to Mableton at 52.7.

70,294 owner-occupied homes, more than the next four counties combined

Cobb brings 131,032 housing units and 70,294 owner-occupied households into our service area, roughly three times the next largest county we cover. It is also one of the furthest, which is the trade we make every time a Cobb job goes on the schedule.

1971 to 2000, and three different deck problems

Cobb housing runs 1971 at Fair Oaks to 2000 at Acworth, a 29 year spread that produces genuinely different work at each end. The oldest end needs rebuilding from the frame. The middle needs repair before something gives. The newest end mostly needs a bigger deck than the builder supplied. Treating those as one job is how a quote goes wrong, so we look at each end on its own terms.

The nine Cobb towns we cover

Marietta, Kennesaw, Smyrna, Acworth, Mableton and Powder Springs are the six we are in most. We work three more besides: Vinings at 2,609 owner-occupied homes on a 1996 median, Austell at 1,959 on a 1993 median, and Fair Oaks at 1,117 on a 1971 median, the oldest housing anywhere in the county.

121 feet to 315 feet

Relief across Cobb runs from 121 feet at Fair Oaks to 315 at Mableton, and Powder Springs at 122 is nearly as flat as Fair Oaks. That means the retaining walls we build in this county are concentrated on the river side rather than spread across it. In the flat west of the county a wall is usually a seat wall or a raised bed, not a structure.

The Chattahoochee, two lakes at Acworth, and a lot of small still water

Cobb carries more named water than any other county we cover: the Chattahoochee inside both Mableton and Smyrna, Allatoona Lake and Lake Acworth inside Acworth, Reeves and Haygood inside Marietta, Cooper Lake, Summerlin Lake and Perkerson Lake at Austell. Kennesaw, Powder Springs and Fair Oaks carry none. That split is the single best predictor of where a screened porch pays for itself, and Noonday, Noses, Nickajack and Olley Creek set the drainage our patios and walkways answer to.

Four incorporated places and two census places

The Cobb mix is the most varied on our whole board: large incorporated cities, a mid-sized town at Austell, and census designated places at Vinings and Fair Oaks. Census places tend to have older, less regular platting than incorporated subdivisions, which changes run length and corner count on a fence job more than the material choice does. Six towns, six fence pages: Marietta, Kennesaw, Smyrna, Acworth, Mableton and Powder Springs.

From 36.9 percent to 82.4 percent owner occupied

This is the number that proves Cobb is not one market. Fair Oaks sits at 36.9 percent owner occupied and Powder Springs at 82.4, a gap of 45 points inside one county line. Pergolas and patio covers follow ownership almost exactly, which is why we put up far more of them at the western end of the county than at the southern end.

Is all of Cobb County covered?

Marietta, Kennesaw, Smyrna, Acworth, Mableton, Powder Springs, Vinings, Austell and Fair Oaks are the nine towns we work in Cobb. It is the furthest county we cover, so work here gets planned rather than dropped in the same week.

Why does the same job vary so much across Cobb?

Because the county spans 29 years of housing age and 45 points of ownership share. A 1971 Fair Oaks deck and a 2000 Acworth deck are different jobs with the same name.
