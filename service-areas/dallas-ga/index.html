Paulding County

Deck Builder in Dallas, GA

Dallas in Paulding County, not Texas. A mid-sized place with early-2000s housing reaching the age where the first real decisions get made.

Roughly 58 minutes, 36.3 road miles by Joe Frank Harris Parkway Northwest and Cartersville Highway.

West across Bartow, not down the interstate

14,714 people in 5,775 homes, 3,004 of them owner occupied. Dallas is the Paulding County town we build a page for, and the route to it runs west across Bartow rather than down the interstate, which is why the drive is longer than the mileage suggests.

Houses from 2002, facing their first serious decision

Half the housing here dates to 2002 or later. At just past twenty, a pressure treated deck is at the point where the choice is real: seal it properly and get another good stretch out of it, or accept that the boards have gone and rebuild the surface on a frame that is usually still fine. Cleaning and sealing is the cheap answer and a rebuild is the permanent one, and which is right depends on what the underside looks like.

Fence work on 5,775 homes

Dallas sits between tight subdivision grid and open acreage, 5,775 homes across the two, and it produces both kinds of fence run. That split is worked through on the Dallas fence page, with materials on the fence company page.

57.6 percent owner occupied

Owner occupancy here runs 57.6 percent, just below the middle of our markets. That is a market where a patio cover is bought for use rather than for show, which usually points at a simple, solid structure over the door people actually walk out of, rather than a feature piece at the back of the lot.

The ordinary Georgia reason to screen a porch

The Dallas city line contains no named lake, pond or river. The reason to screen a porch here is the ordinary Georgia one: from May to September an open porch is a nice idea and a screened one is a room you use.

244 feet across the boundary

Dallas runs 871 to 1,115 feet, a spread of 244 feet, which is almost exactly what neighboring Hiram runs. On that kind of rolling ground the typical retaining wall job is a single terrace that turns a sloping back yard into a flat one, which is the highest-value thing you can do to a lot like this.

Dew Berry Creek, Griffin Creek, Lane Creek

Seven named streams cross the boundary, which is a lot for a place this size. That means most Dallas yards sit somewhere on a drainage path rather than at the top of one, and where a patio or walkway goes has to account for water arriving from uphill as well as falling on the surface itself.
