205,198 people and no city limits

East Cobb has no city limits of its own, so the Census figures on this webpage count its four ZIP codes together: 30062, 30066, 30067 and 30068. Between them they hold 205,198 people in 82,108 homes, by the Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 survey.

Deck repair and replacement in East Cobb: 1984, and decks past forty

East Cobb's housing has a median build year of 1984 across the four ZIP codes, from 1981 in 30068 to 1987 in 30066.

A deck that came with one of those houses, if it's still the original, has been through more than forty Georgia summers. The question at that age is how much of the frame can carry a new surface and how much has to come out, and only the underside answers it.

Deck repair and replacement works through that call, and a deck built new starts from the footings.

Fence installation in East Cobb: 82,108 homes across four ZIP codes

At 82,108 homes, East Cobb holds more housing than any town on this website except Atlanta.

That many yards means a fence job can start from anything: a bare lot, an old fence coming down, a gate where the family already walks. Materials and their upkeep are compared under fence installation.

Pergolas and patio covers in East Cobb: 71.4 percent owner occupied

Owners live in 55,934 of East Cobb's 78,375 occupied homes, a higher share than in Marietta, Smyrna or Kennesaw.

A pergola or a patio cover is something an owner builds once and keeps, so it's worth anchoring and sizing for the long run.

Screened porches in East Cobb: twelve named lakes, all of them small

Twelve named lakes sit inside East Cobb's four ZIP codes, Bishop Lake and Laura Lake among them, and none of the twelve covers more than about 20 acres.

Small, still water is what mosquitoes like best, and a yard within reach of it is the clearest case for a screened porch.

Retaining walls in East Cobb: 252 feet of fall across four ZIP codes

The lowest point we sampled in East Cobb sits at 916 feet and the highest at 1,168.

Where a yard sits on part of that slope, a level lawn or patio usually starts with a retaining wall.

Patios and walkways in East Cobb: Sope Creek, Willeo Creek, Rottenwood Creek

Ten named creeks cross East Cobb, among them Sope Creek, Willeo Creek and Rottenwood Creek.

Water that used to soak into the lawn runs off a new patio instead, so before the base goes down we work out where that water will go, and lay the patio, walkway or steps to send it there.
