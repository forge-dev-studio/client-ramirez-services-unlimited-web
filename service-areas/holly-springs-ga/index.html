Cherokee County

Deck Builder in Holly Springs, GA

Almost nothing in Holly Springs is old enough to need replacing yet, and that changes what the work actually is.

Houses too new to have failed yet

7,312 homes, 5,237 of them owner occupied, and half of them built in 2009 or later. Nothing else we cover is that young. It means the argument that holds almost everywhere else, that the deck is old and the frame is going, is simply not true in Holly Springs, and pretending otherwise would be selling something nobody needs.

2009 houses, and the first deck rather than the third

On houses this new, most Holly Springs decks are original and structurally fine. What people want is not a replacement, it is the thing the builder did not include: a bigger platform, a second level, a stair that goes somewhere sensible, or a deck where there is currently a back door and a drop. That is a new build conversation, and it is the right moment to decide between composite and wood because nothing has to be matched to an existing surface.

7,312 homes on newer platted ground

Nearly all of the 7,312 homes here sit in subdivisions built to one plan, so run lengths and gate counts barely change from street to street. That pattern is worked through on the Holly Springs fence page, with materials on the fence company page.

75.1 percent owner occupied

Three quarters of occupied homes here are owner occupied, second only to Powder Springs. Young houses plus settled owners is the combination that produces the most pergola and patio cover work, because the people involved are finishing a yard they intend to keep rather than patching one they inherited.

Wooded lots, and designing the porch with the deck

There is no named lake, pond or river inside the Holly Springs line. The case for a screened porch here is about wooded lots and evening use, and on a new house it is often cheaper to design the porch and the deck together than to add one to the other later.

293 feet across the boundary

Holly Springs runs 873 to 1,166 feet, 293 feet of change, more than Woodstock and less than Canton. New subdivisions on this kind of ground get cut and filled during construction, so what is left is often a steep back third that never got finished. That is textbook retaining wall territory and the most common first project on a yard like this.

Avery Creek, Badger Creek, Blankets Creek

Four named streams cross the boundary. On recently graded ground the drainage was engineered for the street and the house, not for anything you add behind it, so a new patio, walkway or set of steps has to work inside a scheme somebody else designed. That is easier when the yard is still bare.

Fire pits on a new lot

On a bare new yard the fire pit is often the anchor everything else gets arranged around, which is an argument for deciding it early. The build is hardscape and the designs are in the fire pit guide.

The house is fifteen years old. Is it too early to think about the deck?

No, but it is probably too early to replace it. On housing this new the useful work is usually adding to what is there rather than tearing it out.

Deck first or patio first on a bare yard?

Whichever one the back door opens onto. After that it depends on how much fall there is between the house and the back of the lot.
