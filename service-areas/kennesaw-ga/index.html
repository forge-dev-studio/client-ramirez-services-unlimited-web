Deck Builder in Kennesaw, GA

Kennesaw is a late-nineties city with a high ownership rate, which is the combination that produces the most outdoor work per hundred homes.

About 46 minutes, 35.3 road miles by I-75 and Old 41 Highway.

34,605 people, and most of the houses are owned

Kennesaw is 14,380 homes with 8,753 of them owner occupied, and most of it was built in the late nineties. Owners who stay in a house of that age are the ones who end up rebuilding a deck, not selling around it.

1998, the year the framing starts to talk

Kennesaw houses went up in the nineties, 1998 in the middle of them, which puts the typical original deck at the age where movement starts showing up as noise: a stair that flexes, a rail that gives when you lean on it, a board that cups after a wet spring. None of that is collapse and all of it is the frame telling you something. Deck repair is usually cheaper than people expect at this stage, and a proper clean and seal is what stops the next stage arriving early.

Fence work across 14,380 homes

Almost all of Kennesaw is platted subdivision ground, and what that does to run lengths, corner counts and gate placement is covered on the Kennesaw fence page, with materials on the fence company page.

Nearly two thirds owner occupied

At 64.6 percent, Kennesaw is tied with Mableton for the third highest ownership share of the sixteen towns we cover. That's the market where a pergola or a solid patio cover makes the most sense as an investment, not a splurge, because the person paying for it is the person who will be using it in ten summers.

No lake, and the case for screen anyway

There's no named lake, pond or river inside the city. The case for a screened porch in Kennesaw rests on what screen does for an evening in a wooded subdivision.

264 feet across the boundary

Kennesaw runs from 896 to 1,160 feet. That 264 feet is enough to produce plenty of yards with a usable half and a wasted half, which is the ordinary case for a retaining wall: hold the grade at the break, and the wasted half becomes lawn, patio or a place to put the shed.

Chalker Bridge Creek, John Henry Creek, Noonday Creek

Six named streams cross the city, three of them running through residential ground. Every one is a collection point, and the yards that feed them are the ones where a new hard surface changes something. Setting out a patio, walkway or set of steps starts with which way the ground already falls.

Fire pits, sited on the lot

Siting happens on the lot, once the seating is drawn. Construction sits with hardscape and the options are in the fire pit guide.

My deck feels bouncy but nothing is broken. Is that urgent?

It's not an emergency and it's not nothing. Bounce usually means a joist span or a beam connection that was marginal to start with. It's worth looking at before the next thing loosens.
