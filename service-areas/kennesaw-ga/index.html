34,605 people, and 64.6 percent of them own

Kennesaw holds 14,380 homes and 8,753 of them are owner occupied. High ownership plus late-nineties construction is the profile that generates the most improvement work per household of any market on this list, and the sections below take one measured fact each.

1998, the year the framing starts to talk

The Kennesaw housing median is 1998, which puts the typical original deck at the age where movement starts showing up as noise: a stair that flexes, a rail that gives when you lean on it, a board that cups after a wet spring. None of that is collapse and all of it is the frame telling you something. Deck repair is usually cheaper than people expect at this stage, and a proper clean and seal is what stops the next stage arriving early.

Fence work across 14,380 homes

Kennesaw is a large incorporated place with 14,380 housing units on mostly platted subdivision ground, and what that does to run lengths, corner counts and gate placement is covered on the Kennesaw fence page, with materials on the fence company page.

Nearly two thirds owner occupied

At 64.6 percent, Kennesaw is tied with Mableton for the third highest ownership share of the sixteen hubs. That is the market where a pergola or a solid patio cover makes the most sense as an investment rather than a splurge, because the person paying for it is the person who will be using it in ten summers.

No named water inside the line, and why that is stated plainly

The place boundary contains no named lake, pond or river. There is water nearby but not inside it, and this page will not borrow a neighboring town's lake to make a point. So the case for a screened porch in Kennesaw rests on what screen does for an evening in a wooded subdivision, not on a body of water that is not there.

264 feet across the boundary

Kennesaw runs from 896 to 1,160 feet. That 264 feet is enough to produce plenty of yards with a usable half and a wasted half, which is the ordinary case for a retaining wall: hold the grade at the break, and the wasted half becomes lawn, patio or a place to put the shed.

Chalker Bridge Creek, John Henry Creek, Noonday Creek

Six named streams cross the city, three of them running through residential ground. Every one is a collection point, and the yards that feed them are the ones where a new hard surface changes something. Setting out a patio, walkway or set of steps starts with which way the ground already falls.
