31,481 homes, more than anywhere else we work

Nowhere we work has more homes than Mableton, 31,481 of them, about nineteen times what Adairsville holds. A place that size built over that long holds every fence situation at once: shared subdivision boundaries, older lots with mature trees on the line, and river-side ground where the fall matters. The town name tells you almost nothing about the job. The lot tells you everything.

What went around a back yard in the eighties

Mableton's older half went up before 1988, and what went around a back yard in that decade was chain link, so that is what most original fences here are. Where those are still standing they are often serviceable and ugly, which is a different problem from failing. The usual upgrade path is vinyl or aluminum, and it is worth being clear-eyed about what that buys: appearance and low maintenance, not extra strength.

Trees on the line

On lots this age the boundary is frequently occupied. A mature tree sitting where a post needs to go is the most common thing that turns a straightforward fence into a judgment call, and there are only a few honest options: jog the line, run a short section of a different height, or leave a gap and fill it. Deciding that on the day is how you end up with a fence that looks improvised.
