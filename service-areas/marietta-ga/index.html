62,263 people in 26,783 homes

62,263 people in 26,783 homes, 11,658 of them owner occupied. Marietta is one of the three largest places we cover and it is 42.3 road miles from the shop, which is far enough to be honest about: this is a market for planned work, not for a same-day look at a loose rail.

A 1987 median in a city that keeps rebuilding itself

The midpoint of the Marietta housing stock is 1987. Decks of that generation were framed before composite existed as a mainstream option, so the choice on a rebuild is genuinely open rather than a like-for-like swap. That is worth an actual comparison rather than a default, which is what the composite decking page is for, next to what a new build involves.

Fencing in a city of 26,783 homes

Marietta is a large city carrying 26,783 homes, an enormous spread of lot patterns inside one city line, and the fence consequences of that are set out on the Marietta fence page with the material comparison on the fence company page.

47.2 percent owner occupied, the lowest anywhere we work

Fewer than half the occupied homes in Marietta are owner occupied, the lowest share of the sixteen towns we cover. That does not shrink the market, because 47.2 percent of 24,700 households is still 11,658 of them, but it does change who is buying. A patio cover in Marietta is bought by an owner who has decided to improve rather than move, and that decision is the one that comes first.

Reeves Lake and Haygood Lake

The named water inside the Marietta boundary is two small lakes, Reeves and Haygood, rather than a river. Small still water is the worst kind for mosquitoes, which is why the screened porch argument holds here even though nothing on the map looks dramatic. Screen, screen under a roof and glass are three different rooms, and the page works through what each costs you in light and airflow.

262 feet, and the ridge Marietta sits on

Elevation across Marietta runs 930 to 1,192 feet, the highest starting elevation of any town we cover. The 262 feet of change inside it is mostly the shoulder of that ridge, which produces long, moderate slopes rather than sharp drops. Retaining walls on ground like that are usually about creating a level terrace, not about arresting a collapse.

Noonday Creek, Noses Creek, Olley Creek

Seven named streams cross the city, and Noonday, Noses and Olley Creek are the three that touch the most residential ground. Each of them is a low point that somebody upstream is draining into. Laying a patio or a walkway without knowing which of them your yard feeds is how a hard surface ends up holding water instead of shedding it.
