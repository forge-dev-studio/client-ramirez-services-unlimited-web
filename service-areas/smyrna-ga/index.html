1990, and a deck that has already been patched

Most of Smyrna went up around 1990. Decks of that generation, in a market that turns over as often as this one, have usually been through at least one owner who fixed the visible part. What that leaves is a good-looking surface on questionable framing, which is the most expensive kind of surprise. Repair and replacement starts underneath, and staining and sealing only makes sense once what's under it is sound.

59.4 percent owner occupied

Ownership in Smyrna is 59.4 percent, close to the middle of the towns we cover. On a base this large that's still 14,725 owner-occupied households, so the pergola and patio cover market is substantial in absolute terms even though the share is unremarkable. Infill lots here are often narrow, which usually means an attached cover, not a freestanding one.

The Chattahoochee River and Cooper Lake

Two named waters sit inside the Smyrna boundary, the Chattahoochee and Cooper Lake, one moving and one still. Still water is the one that decides whether an open porch is usable in July, and Cooper Lake is close enough to a lot of housing to matter. What screen, a roof and glass each buy you is on the screened porch page.

259 feet toward the river

Smyrna runs 810 to 1,069 feet. That 259 feet is mostly the drop toward the Chattahoochee, so the retaining wall work clusters on the river side of town. On narrow infill lots the useful wall is often the one that squares up a side yard instead of the back.

Laurel Creek, Nickajack Creek, Poplar Creek

Four named streams cross the boundary, all draining to the river. On a narrow lot with a defined fall, the layout of a patio or a walkway is decided almost entirely by where the water is already going, and fighting that's how you end up with a wet side yard.
