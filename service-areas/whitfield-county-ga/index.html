The wall county

Deck and Fence Builder in Whitfield County, GA

Whitfield has more vertical change in it than any county we cover. If a yard in northwest Georgia needs holding up, the odds are it is here.

Whitfield spans a 38 to 56 minute band, Dalton at 31.2 road miles from us and Varnell at 43.

7,277 owner-occupied homes on the steepest ground we work

Whitfield takes in 14,326 homes and 7,277 owner-occupied households. It also carries 741 feet of relief across the Dalton town boundary, the largest anywhere we work and nearly double the next county.

1982 to 1989

Dalton housing came up in 1982 and Varnell in 1989. The two sit close together, and both are middle-aged: old enough that original decks are at or past the end of their run, young enough that the framing methods are recognizable. On steep ground the posts are longer and the loads are less forgiving, which is why we start repair and replacement here underneath the deck rather than on top of it.

Dalton and Varnell

Dalton is where most of our Whitfield work is. Varnell is the small one, 784 owner-occupied homes, and we cover it just the same. It is 43 road miles out, and at 91.4 percent owner occupied the second most settled place we work after Euharlee. Five named streams cross its boundary, more than Dalton has residential ones.

289 feet to 741 feet

The relief range across Whitfield runs 289 feet at Varnell to 741 at Dalton. Nothing else we cover comes close to the top of that band. This is the county where a retaining wall is structural work rather than landscaping, where the drainage behind it does most of the job, and where skipping that detail is what makes a wall lean three winters later.

No lake and no river, in either town

Neither Dalton nor Varnell has a named lake, pond or river inside its boundary, which is unusual for a county this size. So the reason to screen a porch in Whitfield is elevation and breeze rather than insects coming off standing water. Sixteen named streams across the two towns is what we have to lay patios and walkways out around.

One city, one small town

Whitfield pairs a big city with a small town, which is a wide gap in lot pattern: Dalton runs city blocks, Varnell runs open ground. Running a fence across falling ground is its own problem in both, because a fence follows the grade or it steps down it, and those two look completely different when finished. How that plays out inside the city is on our Dalton fence page.

52.4 to 91.4 percent owner occupied

Dalton at 52.4 percent and Varnell at 91.4 is a 39 point gap across two towns. Nearly all the pergolas and patio covers we build in this county go up at the Varnell end of that, in the settled ground outside the city.

Why do retaining walls lean?

Almost always water. Ground that cannot drain builds pressure behind the wall, and on 741 feet of Whitfield County relief there is a lot of ground above most walls.
