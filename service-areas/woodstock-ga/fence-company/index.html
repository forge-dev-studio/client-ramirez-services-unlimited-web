Fence Company in Woodstock, GA

Woodstock is platted subdivision almost end to end, which makes it the most predictable fence town we work in.

15,371 homes, laid out to a plan

Somebody drew Woodstock's 15,371 homes as rectangles and drew most of them at once. That uniformity is worth something real: side yards repeat, back runs repeat, and the corner count is almost always four. It's the town where an estimate given over the phone comes closest to the estimate given standing in the yard, and where a job finishes on the day it was supposed to.

2005 houses, and the first fence, not the second

Woodstock houses mostly date from 2005 on, so this is first-fence territory, which means the decision is genuinely open, not constrained by what's already there. Wood privacy is the default in subdivisions for good reasons and it has a maintenance bill attached. Vinyl and aluminum trade that bill for a higher start and a different failure mode. Worth comparing properly when nothing has to be matched.

The shared-boundary problem

In a subdivision your fence line is somebody else's fence line on both sides, and often at the back as well. That changes the job in ways nobody mentions: which side the good face goes on, whether the new fence ties into the existing one at the corners, and what happens where the neighbor already has something up. All of it is easier settled before the posts go in than after.

Which side does the good face go on?

Convention puts the finished side facing out. On a shared boundary it's worth agreeing with the neighbor first, because it's the one decision that can't be changed later without taking panels off.
