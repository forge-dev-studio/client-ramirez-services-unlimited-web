Vinyl and Aluminum Fence

Neither of these materials asks for a coat of anything. What they ask for instead is that they go in right the first time.

What you're actually buying

Both of these are bought for the same reason, and it's not the look. It's that neither one ever has to be sanded, stripped, stained or painted. That's worth real money over the life of a fence. It's worth more than money to anyone who has spent a spring weekend on a ladder.

What you give up is flexibility. Wood can be trimmed, patched, extended and refinished on site with a saw.

These are manufactured parts in fixed sizes, and they only go together the way they were designed to.

Vinyl, and the thing it doesn't like

A vinyl privacy panel gives you a solid screen that looks the same in ten summers as on the day it went up. Provided nothing hits it.

Impact is the weakness. A post backed into, a rock thrown by a mower deck or a limb coming down will crack a vinyl rail. Wood in the same spot would dent and carry on. Vinyl doesn't bruise, it breaks.

The compensation is that the break is usually local. Rails slide into pockets routed in the posts, so a damaged bay lifts out and a new one goes in without touching the rest of the run. Heat is the second thing to plan for.

Vinyl expands measurably along a rail on a hot afternoon, which is why the parts float in their pockets, not being screwed tight at both ends.

Aluminum, and why the coating is the product

Ornamental aluminum is bought to be seen through. It marks a boundary, encloses a pool or dresses a front yard without blocking the view. It does none of the work a privacy fence does. The metal itself doesn't rust.

What can fail is the powder coat, and it fails where it has been cut, drilled or scraped instead of across the surface. Careful cutting and finished ends are most of what separates a fence that stays black from one that speckles at every screw.

The other advantage is slopes. Ornamental panels are built to rack, so the rails skew to follow the ground while every picket stays upright. On a yard that falls away, that's the difference between a fence that looks fitted and one that looks stepped.

Where each one is the wrong choice

Vinyl is wrong on a run that takes regular knocks, or where you might want a different color later. It comes in the color it comes in. Aluminum is wrong if the point is privacy, or if a small dog has to be kept in. Picket spacing is generous by design.

Both are wrong on very long agricultural runs, where the cost per foot stops making sense against wood or wire.

What actually happens

Two systems, installed differently

  1. Post spacing isn't negotiable

    Both systems arrive as manufactured panels or rails cut to a fixed length. The posts have to land exactly where the parts expect them, so the whole run is measured and marked before the first hole opens.

  2. Vinyl posts get reinforced where they work hardest

    A hollow vinyl post is fine along a line and not fine at a gate. Gate and corner posts take a steel or aluminum insert so the hinge screws have something solid to bite into.

  3. Aluminum racks to the ground

    Ornamental aluminum panels are built to skew, so the rails follow a slope while the pickets stay vertical. That's set as the panel goes on, not corrected afterward.

  4. Room left for heat

    Vinyl rails grow and shrink with temperature along their length. They sit in routed post pockets with room to move, and a rail fixed rigidly at both ends is a rail that bows in August.

  5. Cut ends finished

    Cutting an aluminum picket or rail exposes bare metal under the powder coat. Those ends get capped or touched in, because that's where corrosion starts on a fence that's otherwise immune to it.

Questions we get asked

Before you call

Is vinyl fence really maintenance free?

It never needs refinishing, which is the claim worth making. It still gets dirty, it still grows a green film on the shaded side, and it still wants washing once or twice a year. Nobody has to stand on a ladder with a brush, which is the actual benefit.

Does vinyl go brittle or yellow?

Older and thinner product did both. Panels with UV stabilizer through the material, not just on the surface hold their color far better. Impact is the real weakness: a mower stone or a reversing trailer cracks vinyl where wood would dent.

Can a broken vinyl panel be replaced on its own?

Usually yes, and it's one of the better arguments for the system. The rails slot into the posts, so a damaged section comes out without disturbing the run either side. Matching color on an older fence is the harder part.

Why choose aluminum over steel?

Aluminum doesn't rust. Steel does, and once a coating is broken on steel the corrosion spreads under it. Aluminum is lighter, which makes long panels easier to hang square, and powder coat on aluminum is the finish that outlasts everything else on this page.

Which one is right around a pool?

Aluminum, in almost every case. It's open, it doesn't corrode in a chlorinated splash zone, and self-closing self-latching gate hardware is standard for it. We fit the gate hardware you specify.
